# Puzzle Mode

Puzzle Mode is a single-player challenge where you maximize your score by finding the best kanji combinations from a randomly dealt set of cards.

## Setup

A puzzle is generated by randomly dealing:
- **5 neta cards** (from the pool of 10 unique neta cards)
- **6 shari cards** (from the pool of 46 shari cards)

These represent your "hand" and the "table" respectively.

## Objective

Find the combination of kanji that maximizes your total score (sum of stroke counts).

You may form **up to 5 kanji** (one per neta card), but you're limited by:
- Each neta card can only be used once
- Each shari card can only be used once
- Only valid kanji from the approved list count

## Scoring

- Each valid kanji scores its **total stroke count**
- Your puzzle score is the sum of all kanji you successfully form
- Higher stroke count kanji are more valuable

## Challenge Variants

### Standard Puzzle
- Form as many valid kanji as possible
- Maximize total stroke count
- No time limit

### Timed Puzzle
- Same rules as Standard
- Complete within a time limit (e.g., 60 seconds, 120 seconds)
- Tests both knowledge and quick thinking

### Target Score
- Given a target score to reach or exceed
- Find any combination that meets the target
- Multiple solutions may exist

### Minimalist
- Form exactly N kanji (e.g., "Form exactly 3 kanji")
- Maximize score with that constraint
- Tests strategic selection

## Example Puzzle

**Your neta cards:** 亻 氵 扌 木 言

**Shari cards on table:** 可 木 寺 青 売 己

**Possible solutions:**
- 何 (亻+ 可) = 7 strokes
- 活 (氵+ 舌) = ... wait, 舌 isn't available
- 持 (扌+ 寺) = 9 strokes
- 村 (木 + 寸) = ... 寸 isn't available
- 記 (言 + 己) = 10 strokes

One valid solution: 何 + 持 + 記 = 26 strokes

Can you find a better combination?

## Why Puzzles Always Have Solutions

The neta and shari decks are designed so that every neta card can combine with multiple shari cards to form valid kanji. With 5 neta cards and 6 shari cards dealt, there will statistically always be at least one valid combination available.

However, the *optimal* solution varies greatly:
- Some puzzles may only allow 1-2 valid kanji
- Others might allow all 5 neta cards to be used
- The maximum achievable score depends entirely on the random deal

This variability is what makes each puzzle unique and interesting.
